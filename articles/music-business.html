MUSIC BUSINESS  ·  HOW IT WORKS

From finished track to distribution

A practical planning outline for independent artists preparing to release music digitally.

By [AUTHOR NAME] · Updated [MONTH YEAR]

Research template: This page does not compare or endorse a distributor. CD Baby, DistroKid, and other services change their fees, terms, features, and eligibility rules; check each provider’s current official information before publishing.

Get the release ready

Make a checklist for final masters, artwork, track titles, songwriter and producer credits, explicit-content labels, and ownership or permissions. Requirements can vary by platform and release type.

Compare distribution services

Research the current pricing model, release fees, commission or royalty terms, payout thresholds, supported stores, takedown policies, customer support, and add-on costs. Compare the same criteria across providers and link directly to official terms.

Plan the rollout

Set a realistic release date, prepare a clean press kit, coordinate collaborators, and plan consistent promotion. Don’t promise playlist placement, streams, or revenue outcomes.

Promotion beyond release day

Consider short-form clips, email lists, local press, live shows, creator partnerships, and community engagement. Explain what you have actually tried and avoid claiming guaranteed results.

Before you sign

Read the provider’s current contract and account terms. This article is general information, not legal, financial, or career advice.
